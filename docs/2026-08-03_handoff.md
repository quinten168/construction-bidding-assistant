## The six mapping decisions — settled, do not re-litigate

Verbatim from the owner, 2026-08-03:

1. **Upsert clobber: APPROVED.** Add the `row_source` guard to the CI upsert. Preventing the
   overwrite by construction beats detecting it after. Accepted that CI can no longer update a
   `plan_reader`-owned row.
2. **City sentinel: `UNKNOWN`**, uppercase, matching the key's casing. Never an empty string, which
   gives `MN_STEARNS__2024-06-07` with a double delimiter that is ambiguous and hard to grep.
3. **Unparseable `bid_date`: never reject the row.** Write `1900-01-01` plus a provenance column
   marking it unparsed. Obviously fake, sorts to the bottom, easy to find later.
4. **`ON DELETE CASCADE`:** the write path is **upsert-only, no `DELETE` against `bid_projects`
   ever**, including in dedupe — and specifically not delete-then-insert. Changing the FK to
   `RESTRICT` is Quinten's call (#55); do not change it.
5. **Unit columns: keep all 8.** One global `unit_source` cannot say *which* quantity's unit was
   assumed, and that is not recoverable later.
6. **19 → 58 columns: approved.** Both preconditions checked and clean — `ci_db.py:32` uses
   `row_factory=dict_row` so nothing is positional, and `pull_scratch_updates.py:238` uses an
   explicit `select {cols}` list, not `select *`. The CI feature builder's `.iloc[0]` calls are
   *row* selection followed by `.get(key)` by name.

Also settled: `project_natural_key` = `{state}_{county}_{city}_{bid_date}`, city from reverse
geocode. **Accepted risk, recorded:** a geocoded city is the nearest locality to a road midpoint, not
necessarily the city the DOT used, so some keys will not match how historical rows were keyed. Not
reconciling against historicals; it is a natural key and can be recomputed table-wide with an
`UPDATE` once composition is settled with Quinten.
